Dissertation

The Health–Environment Tradeoff in U.S. Diets: Inequity Across Generations and Space

U.S. diets shape population health and the climate through their joint effects on nutrition, cardiometabolic risk, and greenhouse-gas emissions, and these impacts are distributed unequally across socioeconomic groups, generations, and places. The dissertation asks who bears the health and environmental burdens of current diets, how dietary patterns have shifted across birth cohorts, and where local food environments amplify or mitigate risk. It draws on the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), food-level emission factors, and age–period–cohort and multilevel models to identify dietary changes that are both effective and equitable.

Committee: Giovanni Baiocchi (chair), Laixiang Sun, Taylor Oshan, Pan He (University of Hong Kong), Edmond Shenassa (UMD School of Public Health). Expected May 2027.

Peer-Reviewed Publications

  1. When healthier eating emits more: Socioeconomic divergence in U.S. diet sustainability

    Wen Qi and Giovanni Baiocchi

    Journal of Cleaner Production, 2026, 575: 149143.

    JournalDOI

    Summary

    Using nationally representative NHANES dietary data, we combine the Healthy Eating Index with diet-related greenhouse-gas emissions across 13 food components to characterize how actually consumed U.S. diets balance nutritional quality against climate impact. Diets rich in seafood and plant protein, fruit, and whole grains achieve a favorable trade-off, whereas diets high in saturated fat, sodium, and refined grains add environmental burden with little health benefit. The trade-off diverges by socioeconomic position: diet quality responds mainly to income, emissions respond to prices mostly within more expensive diets, and education improves both only at its highest level. Policies that make nutritious, low-impact foods affordable are needed to close the gap.

Under Review

  1. Younger birth cohorts have poorer diets but greater health gains from plant-rich dietary shifts

    Wen Qi, Laixiang Sun, Giovanni Baiocchi, and Edmond Shenassa

    Under review

    Summary

    Using age–period–cohort models on NHANES 1999–2020 (about 26,000 adults), we separate generational differences in diet quality, cardiometabolic dysfunction, and diet-related greenhouse-gas emissions from age and period effects, then simulate individual-level, nutritionally constrained shifts of 20–50% toward EAT-Lancet reference diets. Younger cohorts start from poorer diets but gain the most in cardiometabolic health; older adults see smaller gains and potential protein-quality trade-offs; emissions fall across all cohorts. The results argue for cohort-aware dietary policy that pairs sustainability targets with age-appropriate nutrition safeguards.

Working Papers

  1. Nutrition-blind shelves: Auditing U.S. online baby food against the WHO Nutrient and Promotion Profile Model

    Wen Qi and Giovanni Baiocchi

    In preparation

    Summary

    We audit 1,234 unique commercial foods for infants and young children sold online by six U.S. retailers (Amazon, Target, Whole Foods, Harris Teeter, Safeway, and Costco) against the composition and promotion criteria of the WHO Nutrient and Promotion Profile Model. Six in ten products fail at least one composition criterion. Organic certification and the premium brand tier are associated with fewer added-sugar failures, but no visible cue or label marks overall compliance, and the promotional claims that cover almost every listing carry price premiums within brand without being associated with compliance. The study links large-scale retail data collection, LLM-assisted claim coding with author adjudication, and brand-clustered inference.

  2. Misalignment between acute myocardial infarction burden and access to certified emergency care in the United States: A national spatial analysis and network optimization study

    Wen Qi, Yao Li, Giovanni Baiocchi, Laixiang Sun, Renee Y. Hsia, Yu-Chu Shen, and Edmond Shenassa

    In preparation

    Summary

    We estimate ZIP-level demand for acute myocardial infarction care from Medicare claims and hospital service-area flows, and match it against the national supply of hospitals with interventional cardiac catheterization capability. Two-step floating catchment accessibility at 30- and 60-minute thresholds identifies both under-served areas and over-served clusters, and location–allocation models show where added or reallocated capacity would yield the largest gains in timely access.

  3. Fine-tuned language models for food-processing classification and the GroceryDB extension

    Matej Sebek, Wen Qi, Anurag Jain, Fiona Nicholi, Gordana Ispirova, and Giulia Menichetti

    In preparation Channing Division of Network Medicine, Brigham and Women’s Hospital / Harvard Medical School

    Summary

    Extends GroceryDB, a research database of the U.S. grocery supply, with more than 100,000 product records collected from six national retailers, and retrains the fine-tuned BERT and XGBoost classifiers behind the food-processing score (FPro) on the expanded data, improving held-out discrimination and aligning the predicted distribution of processing with what is observed on retail shelves.

  4. Food complexity and ultra-processed food prevalence across the world

    Wen Qi and Giovanni Baiocchi

    In preparation

    Summary

    Builds a country–year panel that links the complexity and import dependence of national food systems, derived from trade and price data, to the prevalence of processed and ultra-processed foods and to population obesity, to ask how the structure of a country’s food supply shapes its dietary transition.
