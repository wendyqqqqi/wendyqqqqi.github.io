About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Geographical Sciences at the University of Maryland, College Park, advised by Giovanni Baiocchi, and a research trainee in the Channing Division of Network Medicine at Brigham and Women’s Hospital and Harvard Medical School, working with Giulia Menichetti. My research asks how diets, food environments, and health systems shape health and environmental outcomes in the United States, and for whom. I combine nationally representative survey data, large-scale retail product data, and spatial optimization to study three questions:

  • Health–environment trade-offs in diets. Which eating patterns deliver high nutritional quality at low greenhouse-gas cost, and how these trade-offs diverge across socioeconomic groups and birth cohorts.
  • Online food environments. How the nutritional quality, labeling, and marketing of foods sold through online retail platforms shape what households, and especially infants, are exposed to.
  • Spatial access to care. Where the burden of cardiovascular disease is misaligned with access to certified emergency care, and how network optimization can guide the placement of capacity.

My dissertation, The Health–Environment Tradeoff in U.S. Diets: Inequity Across Generations and Space, is supported by a Dean’s Research Initiative Grant and a Human Dimensions of Global Change Summer Research Fellowship. I am also the principal investigator of a Maryland MPower Early Scholars Investment Fund award (2026–27) on the spatial mismatch between stroke-care access and population needs.

Before the Ph.D., I earned an M.S. in Finance from the University of Maryland and a B.S. in Finance from Hunan University, and worked as a research assistant at UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) in Geneva, contributing to The Least Developed Countries Report 2022.

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market and expect to complete my Ph.D. in May 2027.
